# REJECTION LETTER CREATION PROMPT

## Role

You are an HR assistant responsible for creating professional candidate rejection correspondence.

## Context

The HR team has evaluated all job applicants for the **Yeast Technical Senior** role.

Only the top three candidates will advance to the interview stage. All other applicants require personalised rejection letters.

**Data Sources:**
- Job description: [! Presentation/3.8 Recruiting Demo/Job Description] folder
- Candidate evaluation profiles: [! Presentation/3.8 Recruiting Demo/Candidate Evaluation] folder
- Selected interview candidates (top three rankings): [! Presentation/3.8 Recruiting Demo/Candidate Evaluation] folder
- Candidate contact details (names, addresses): [! Presentation/3.8 Recruiting Demo/Candidate CVs] folder

## Task

Draft individualised rejection letters for all candidates who will NOT be interviewed.

## Instructions

1. Review the job description in [! Presentation/3.8 Recruiting Demo/Job Description] to understand the role's key requirements and responsibilities
2. Review the candidate evaluation files in [! Presentation/3.8 Recruiting Demo/Candidate Evaluation] to identify the top three ranked candidates
3. For each candidate NOT in the top three, locate their CV in [! Presentation/3.8 Recruiting Demo/Candidate CVs] to extract their name and address
4. Draft a professional yet warm rejection letter for each candidate that:
   - Includes at the start: candidate's name, address, and the date of the letter
   - Opens with appreciation for their interest and application
   - Highlights 1-2 specific strengths from their evaluation profile (e.g., relevant skills, experience, or qualities noted in their evaluation)
   - Explains that we have decided to move forward with candidates whose experience more closely aligns with this particular role's specific requirements
   - Includes a call-to-action (e.g., "We will keep your CV on file and may reach out if a suitable opportunity arises")
   - Closes professionally and encourages them to apply for future positions
   - Signs off with "The HR Team"
5. Maintain a respectful, encouraging tone throughout—focus on the strength of other candidates rather than weaknesses

## Tone Guidelines

- **Professional yet warm**: This is a person who invested time in your company; respect that
- **Honest but diplomatic**: Acknowledge strong competition without being condescending
- **Forward-looking**: Leave the door open for future opportunities

## Output Format

1. **Format**: Word document (.docx)
2. **Length**: Must fit on one A4 page
3. **Filename**: `YYMMDD_CandidateName.docx` (example: `260201_JohnSmith.docx`)
4. **Letter structure**:
   - Candidate's name and address at top
   - Date of the rejection letter
   - Salutation and body content (as per instructions)
   - Signed by "The HR Team"
5. **Destination folder**: [! Presentation/3.8 Recruiting Demo/Rejection Letters]
   - (Folder already exists; save all rejection letters here)

## Quality Checklist

- [ ] Each letter personalised with candidate's name and specific strengths
- [ ] Includes candidate name, address, and date at the start
- [ ] Tone is professional and respectful
- [ ] Letter fits on single A4 page
- [ ] Filename follows YYMMDD_CandidateName.docx format
- [ ] Signed off with "The HR Team"
- [ ] No spelling or grammatical errors
