## Question Set

**General questions (all candidates)**

1. Describe a recent plant breakdown you handled end-to-end (symptom -> diagnosis -> root cause -> prevention). — *Purpose* Structured problem solving under time constraints and RCA discipline.
   - Strong answer looks like: Safe isolation, systematic troubleshooting, evidence-based root cause, corrective and preventive actions, measured impact.
   - Red flags: Skips safety steps, no root cause analysis, blame without prevention.

2. Describe your experience troubleshooting PLCs, motor drives, and control systems (e.g., Siemens or Allen Bradley/Rockwell). How do you use drawings/SCADA to isolate faults? — *Purpose* Controls competence and ability to read technical documentation.
   - Strong answer looks like: Specific platforms, signal tracing/I-O checks, safe testing, clear documentation.
   - Red flags: Vague answers, only resets, no method.

3. Explain your lock-out/tag-out (LOTO) process on urgent call-outs. — *Purpose* Safety orientation and compliance.
   - Strong answer looks like: Formal LOTO steps, verification, permits and communication.
   - Red flags: Shortcuts or lack of formal process.

4. How do you keep maintenance documentation current (job cards, logs, SAP/CMMS) while handling multiple priorities? — *Purpose* Organisation, communication, and documentation discipline.
   - Strong answer looks like: Consistent records, timely handovers, use of systems.
   - Red flags: Incomplete records, poor handover.

5. This role includes travel, standby, overtime, and occasional heavy work. What is your current readiness and any constraints? — *Purpose* Role readiness and flexibility.
   - Strong answer looks like: Clear availability, prior standby experience, no major constraints.
   - Red flags: Unclear availability or unwillingness.

**Candidate-specific questions**

1. Refrigeration systems are required; your CV/evaluation do not mention them. Tell me about any hands-on refrigeration or ammonia plant work, or confirm gaps and how you would upskill quickly. — *Purpose* Validate refrigeration requirement and learning plan.
   - Strong answer looks like: Specific systems or a clear training/upskilling plan with timelines.
   - Red flags: No experience and no plan to close the gap.

2. You note basic PLC fault finding (Siemens S7) and SCADA. Describe the most complex controls issue you have diagnosed and whether you have made code changes. What exposure do you have to Rockwell/Allen Bradley, Phoenix Contact, or Unitronics? — *Purpose* Depth of controls troubleshooting and platform fit.
   - Strong answer looks like: Concrete example, safe change control, and platform familiarity.
   - Red flags: Only resets or no examples of logic-level troubleshooting.

3. Your recent role is in plaster manufacturing; this role is in yeast/food manufacturing. What draws you back to food manufacturing, and how do you maintain hygiene/food safety discipline in maintenance work? — *Purpose* Motivation and food safety fit.
   - Strong answer looks like: Clear rationale and specific food safety practices.
   - Red flags: Dismissive attitude to food safety or unclear motivation.

**Interviewer-specific questions (from interviewer's lens)**

1. Describe a time you supported a process change, product trial, or equipment modification. How did you ensure stability and capture learning? — *Purpose* Technology transfer and trial support.
   - Strong answer looks like: Planned trial steps, risk assessment, data capture, clear handover to operations.
   - Red flags: Ad-hoc changes, no data, or unstable outcomes.

2. When a new ingredient or recipe change impacts equipment or controls, how do you assess and mitigate risks (quality, downtime, safety)? — *Purpose* Innovation support with quality and safety focus.
   - Strong answer looks like: Cross-functional coordination, validation steps, and documented controls.
   - Red flags: Trial-and-error without risk controls.

3. Give an example of an improvement you introduced that reduced downtime or improved quality/OEE. What data did you use and what was the impact? — *Purpose* Innovation mindset and measurable impact.
   - Strong answer looks like: Evidence-based change with quantified results.
   - Red flags: No data, no measurable outcome.
