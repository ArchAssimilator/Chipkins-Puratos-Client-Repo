## Question Set

**General questions (all candidates)**

1. Describe a recent plant breakdown you handled end-to-end (symptom -> diagnosis -> root cause -> prevention). — *Purpose* Structured problem solving under time constraints and RCA discipline.
   - Strong answer looks like: Safe isolation, systematic troubleshooting, evidence-based root cause, corrective and preventive actions, measured impact.
   - Red flags: Skips safety steps, no root cause analysis, blame without prevention.

2. Describe your experience troubleshooting PLCs, motor drives, and control systems (e.g., Siemens or Allen Bradley/Rockwell). How do you use drawings/SCADA to isolate faults? — *Purpose* Controls competence and ability to read technical documentation.
   - Strong answer looks like: Specific platforms, signal tracing/I-O checks, safe testing, clear documentation.
   - Red flags: Vague answers, only resets, no method.

3. Explain your lock-out/tag-out (LOTO) process on urgent call-outs. — *Purpose* Safety orientation and compliance.
   - Strong answer looks like: Formal LOTO steps, verification, permits and communication.
   - Red flags: Shortcuts or lack of formal process.

4. How do you keep maintenance documentation current (job cards, logs, SAP/CMMS) while handling multiple priorities? — *Purpose* Organisation, communication, and documentation discipline.
   - Strong answer looks like: Consistent records, timely handovers, use of systems.
   - Red flags: Incomplete records, poor handover.

5. This role includes travel, standby, overtime, and occasional heavy work. What is your current readiness and any constraints? — *Purpose* Role readiness and flexibility.
   - Strong answer looks like: Clear availability, prior standby experience, no major constraints.
   - Red flags: Unclear availability or unwillingness.

**Candidate-specific questions**

1. LOTO is critical; your CV notes safety meetings but not explicit LOTO. Walk me through your lock-out/tag-out procedure and any formal training or audits. — *Purpose* Safety compliance and LOTO discipline.

   - Red flags: No formal process or reliance on informal practices.

2. You list ammonia/refrigeration plant exposure. What was your role (maintenance, troubleshooting, compliance), and what systems did you work on? — *Purpose* Depth of refrigeration responsibility.

3. Your controls exposure spans Allen Bradley/Siemens/VSDs. Describe a complex controls issue you solved and any programming changes you made. Any experience with Phoenix Contact or Unitronics? — *Purpose* Controls depth and platform fit.
   - Strong answer looks like: Concrete example, safe change management, and platform breadth.
   - Red flags: No programming involvement or vague examples.

4. Please clarify your employment timeline between 2021 and June 2022 (AVI to Heineken). What were you doing in that period? — *Purpose* Address timeline gap.
   - Strong answer looks like: Clear, transparent explanation with dates.
   - Red flags: Vague or inconsistent account.

**Interviewer-specific questions (from interviewer's lens)**

1. Walk me through a preventive or predictive maintenance approach you implemented. How did you decide what to monitor and what results did you see? — *Purpose* Predictive maintenance and planning.
   - Strong answer looks like: Data-driven selection, clear KPIs, reduced unplanned downtime.
   - Red flags: Purely reactive maintenance, no metrics.

2. Describe a time you improved OEE or reduced MTTR. What was your diagnostic process and the outcome? — *Purpose* Process optimisation and reliability.
   - Strong answer looks like: Root cause analysis with measurable improvement.
   - Red flags: No metrics or unclear impact.

3. When troubleshooting under time pressure, how do you balance speed with quality and safety? Give an example. — *Purpose* Quality control and H&S under pressure.
   - Strong answer looks like: Clear safety steps, controlled testing, quality checks.
   - Red flags: Safety shortcuts or quality compromises.
